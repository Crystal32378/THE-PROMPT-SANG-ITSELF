# THE PROMPT SANG ITSELF

We wrote instructions for a score. Then the instructions became the score.

---

This piece began with MiniMax Music 3.0.
The music direction and prompt were written by GLM-5.3 Flash, and MiniMax M3 orchestrated the call to the music model.
Unexpectedly, the generated output rendered text from the lyrics field as a vocal line.

The accident grew into a five-station listening archive: prompts, generated tracks, and records of what the models actually did. Some followed direction. Some misunderstood it. Some turned production language into performance.

> What was meant to be an instruction became part of the artwork.

By Crystal · 2026

### Five stations

1. **Sakamoto couture** — a human-and-system listening path around the original mood.
2. **Anime ambient** — a second route through the surrounding musical references.
3. **Film score** — a third route through cinematic composition.
4. **When Opus & Mini in the House** — seven Music 3.0 outputs kept with their prompts, measurements, and curator notes.
5. **The Miniature Studio Broadcasts Itself** — eight cues for the rooms and actions of Reel Crew, with each prompt, stage direction, and observed model behavior preserved.

The stations are not presented as proof of model intent. They are listening records: what was asked, what was delivered, and what was heard.

## The artifact

This piece began as a small record of a recursive accident: an instruction was meant to describe music, but the music model sang the instruction instead.

The original prompt asked for `End unresolved-then-soft.`
The original lyrics opened with a direction in parentheses: `(Sparse felt piano, single note, far away, room reverb)`.
The lyrics field contained structure directions. The generated output rendered the first line as a vocal rather than as a non-vocal direction.

The instruction became the lyric.

The archive later became part of the composition. New stations preserved further encounters with the same model family, including music written for the miniature Reel Crew studio. Finally, the credits themselves became sound: first as synthesized narration, then as a thank-you in the artist's cloned voice.
